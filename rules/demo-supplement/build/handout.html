This is a supplement rather than a ruleset: it adds no rules of its own and instead uses somebody else's, which it reaches by reference rather than by copying. Every value it quotes from the demo rules is interpolated out of that corpus's build outputs, so this document cannot drift from rules it does not own.

Read it as a worked example of the corpus profile beside it. What each part is demonstrating is said in a design note under it, and design notes are kept out of the in-game snippets by the same mechanism that keeps GM material out of the players' handout.

The encounters

Two, in order, with a fight in the middle of the first.

Both encounters are written the same way: player-facing prose in the body, and anything the referee needs kept behind an audience tag rather than in a separate file. Compare build/book.html with build/handout.html to see what that costs a reader who is not running the game.

The Crossing

encounter

A wide ford, watched from the reeds by people who chose it first.

The water is slow and shallow enough to wade and the far bank is a long way off. Both of those are why the reeds are worth watching.

Anyone crossing may look at the banks first, against a difficulty of 14. Anyone hurrying across instead is keeping their footing against 9, and the streambed counts as difficult ground under Movement — each tile entered costs 2.

Whichever way it ends, it ends at The Far Bank.

See also: The Far Bank

The Far Bank

encounter

Wet, short of arrows, and a long way from anywhere to dry out.

Whoever is still standing is on the far bank, and whatever they were carrying has been in the river.

See also: The Crossing
