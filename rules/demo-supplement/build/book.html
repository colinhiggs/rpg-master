This is a supplement rather than a ruleset: it adds no rules of its own and instead uses somebody else's, which it reaches by reference rather than by copying. Every value it quotes from the demo rules is interpolated out of that corpus's build outputs, so this document cannot drift from rules it does not own.

Read it as a worked example of the corpus profile beside it. What each part is demonstrating is said in a design note under it, and design notes are kept out of the in-game snippets by the same mechanism that keeps GM material out of the players' handout.

The encounters

Two, in order, with a fight in the middle of the first.

Both encounters are written the same way: player-facing prose in the body, and anything the referee needs kept behind an audience tag rather than in a separate file. Compare build/book.html with build/handout.html to see what that costs a reader who is not running the game.

The Crossing

encounter

A wide ford, watched from the reeds by people who chose it first.

The water is slow and shallow enough to wade and the far bank is a long way off. Both of those are why the reeds are worth watching.

Anyone crossing may look at the banks first, against a difficulty of 14. Anyone hurrying across instead is keeping their footing against 9, and the streambed counts as difficult ground under Movement — each tile entered costs 2.

Running it

The scouts open and the captain does not, until somebody is in the water. If the party spotted the reeds, reverse that: the captain has nothing to gain by waiting for an ambush that is not going to happen.

Whichever way it ends, it ends at The Far Bank.

Design note

Three things here have no equivalent in a ruleset. This document is called encounter.md and sits in a directory called the-crossing, which is where its id comes from: the encounter kind declares id_from: directory, for a corpus that files one document per directory alongside that document's own maps and handouts. The guarantee is the one the filename rule gives everywhere else -- an id is derivable from where the document sits, so a document cannot be moved without its links noticing.

The block is called setup rather than mechanics, and the prose interpolates out of it by name; a block is addressed by the name its kind declares, so the toolset never has to be told what an encounter is.

And the checks are a list, addressed by each entry's own id rather than by position — write setup.checks.spot-the-ford.difficulty, not setup.checks.0.difficulty, so that adding a check at the top of the list does not silently repoint every number in the paragraph below it.

Both difficulties are covered by the drift check exactly as a rule's values are: write one of them out as a digit and the build fails.

The captain is linked from inside the gm-only span above and nowhere else, which is why build/book.html lists it under "See also" here and build/handout.html lists nothing but the far bank. A related-links line is built per target, from the links still standing after that target's audience policy — otherwise the handout would name the one document the target exists to withhold, and hang a dead anchor off it.

See also: Captain, The Far Bank

The Far Bank

encounter

Wet, short of arrows, and a long way from anywhere to dry out.

Whoever is still standing is on the far bank, and whatever they were carrying has been in the river.

Going back across is a question about the weather rather than about the Tarn, which is passable at the ford below 30 inches and rises within 6 hours of rain on the moor.

Running it

There is nothing left to fight here. What there is instead is a decision about whether to go back for anything, and it should cost something either way.

Design note

This document carries no data block at all, which is allowed: a kind says which blocks its documents may carry, not which they must. It is here because The Crossing names it in frontmatter — setup.leads_to is declared as holding a document id, so an encounter pointing at an encounter that does not exist is a build error rather than something discovered at the table.

It is also where the second kind of reference is used. river-tarn is not a document in this corpus and is not in demo either: it comes from refs/almanac/, a vendored corpus — a committed copy of what that corpus published, rather than a path to a checkout of it. Nothing in the paragraph above says so, which is the point. A reference is a reference, and where the outputs came from is a question about this repository rather than about the prose.

What differs is what gets recorded and what a link can do. Because the copy carries a VENDORED.json stamp, _references in the built outputs says which revision of 3.1.0 this was built against and not merely the version. And because only the data was vendored and not the book, the reference declares no href, so the link renders as a span carrying data-rule-id rather than as an anchor into a book nobody has. A dead anchor would be worse than no anchor.

The opposition

Scout

foe

Fast, cowardly, and there to be seen a moment too late.

A scout is the cheapest thing that can still make a crossing expensive. It carries sling, knife and no armour worth the name.

FieldValue
Threat2
Attack5
Hit points6
Speed tiles7

It moves 7 tiles, which is further than the 4 tiles a character gets under Movement — so a scout that decides to leave has left.

Design note

This document is the base of an inheritance chain: the captain below declares only what differs. Note also the two references reaching into the demo corpus — a link and an interpolation — neither of which needs any syntax of its own. A document is addressed the same way whether it is in this corpus or in one this corpus references.

Captain

foe

The one that has to die before the rest of them will run.

A captain is a scout that has survived long enough to take something better off somebody. It keeps the scout's reflexes and loses the scout's willingness to leave.

FieldValue
Threat4
Attack5
Hit points13
Speed tiles7

Its attack of 5 is inherited rather than declared — this document's frontmatter says nothing about it, and changing the scout changes it here. Its shield, sword replace the scout's outright, because a list is a statement about a whole set: a captain carrying a shield and a sword is not also still carrying a sling.

Design note: inheritance, and when it is resolved

based_on merges block by block. Scalars replace, nested maps merge key by key, lists replace wholesale. It is resolved before anything reads the data, which is why the drift linter catches prose here restating an INHERITED value as readily as a declared one — try writing the attack value out as a literal and the build fails.

Notes for the referee

note

Everything in this document is for whoever is running the game, and none of it is tagged: the note kind declares audience: gm-only in the corpus profile, so the whole body carries that audience without a single marker in the file.

The consequence is worth being precise about. A target that keeps gm-only renders this document. A target that drops it does not render it at all — an empty section with a heading and nothing under it would tell a reader there was something here to miss, which is the opposite of what an audience is for. Look at build/handout.html: this document has no entry in the contents and no anchor in the body.

The scouts in The Crossing are meant to be seen a moment too late rather than never; if the party has spotted them, let them see them.
